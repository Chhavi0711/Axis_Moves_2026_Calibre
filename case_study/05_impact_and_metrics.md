# 05 - Impact and Metrics

**Important framing note:** Axis Calibre is a proposal at Round 3 of a business
challenge, not a piloted or shipped system. Nothing below is a measured result - it's
the impact logic we designed toward, and what we'd expect a pilot to validate or correct.
We've kept this distinction explicit throughout the repo rather than presenting
directional goals as achieved outcomes.

## The headline comparison we used

**~12 days → same-day**, illustrated through the Anjali case: not because any step is
rushed, but because the case reaches the right person immediately instead of climbing a
ladder it didn't need to climb.

## Why it compounds - the four-sided value logic

We deliberately framed impact across four stakeholders rather than a single metric,
because the brief asks for business impact across dimensions like revenue, productivity,
cost, risk, and employee experience - a single "speed" number wouldn't capture why this
is a durable advantage rather than a one-time efficiency gain.

| Stakeholder | Metric we'd track | What it captures |
|---|---|---|
| **Customer** | Speed to Decision | Time from request to a final, actionable decision |
| **Axis (the Bank)** | Decision Quality Score | Whether cases are landing with the person statistically best placed to judge them |
| **Employee** | Rising Talent Rate | How quickly strong judgment becomes visible and rewarded, independent of tenure |
| **Organization** | Memory Reuse Rate | How much each decision's outcome improves the next match - i.e., whether institutional judgment is compounding rather than resetting with staff turnover |

The combined effect we're arguing for is **top-talent retention** - people who feel
their judgment is recognized and used, not gated behind a title, are less likely to
leave for an organization that will let them decide sooner.

## Baseline assumptions we'd want a pilot to test

Being upfront about what we assumed rather than measured:

- That a meaningful share of the ~12-day delay in cases like Anjali's is attributable to
  routing through unnecessary approval layers, not to genuine additional scrutiny at each
  layer
- That decision-outcome data (approved/declined, and how the case performed afterward)
  is available and clean enough, across regions, to build a reliable Calibre score
  within a 2–3 region SME lending pilot window
- That "capacity" (current caseload) can be measured reliably enough to factor into
  matching without creating new bottlenecks of its own
- That a dual-run pilot (new system alongside the existing grid) generates enough
  parallel cases to compare speed and quality meaningfully within a reasonable pilot
  window

## What "success" would look like at the pilot stage

Rather than inventing a specific percentage target (which nothing in our current
material supports), we'd frame pilot success as:

- Cases in the dual-run consistently reaching a decision faster via Calibre-routing than
  via the existing grid, for cases of comparable complexity
- No increase in decision reversals, disputes, or compliance flags versus the existing
  grid over the same period
- Early, directional signal on Rising Talent Rate - i.e., whether officers below senior
  titles are being matched to, and successfully deciding, cases they wouldn't have
  touched under the old ladder

This is the section we'd expect to gain real numbers in future rounds (Round 4 / the
finale), once - and if - a pilot design or simulation produces them.
