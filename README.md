# Axis Calibre - Redesigning How Axis Bank Makes Its Most Important Decisions

**Axis MOVES 2026** | Transformation Council Challenge | Axis Bank × InsideKampus
**Status:** Advanced to Round 3 (Short PPT Submission) - updated as the team progresses

> A proposal to make decision authority at Axis Bank earned by demonstrated judgment,
> not fixed by title - so the best decision reaches the right person immediately,
> without skipping any check the Bank relies on today.

---

## The Problem

Anjali, a merchant, lands her biggest export order and urgently needs working capital.
Her Relationship Manager is confident the loan should be approved - but the request
still has to climb through multiple approval layers, because authority is tied to
designation, not to who is actually best-placed to judge the case.

**Twelve days later, the decision comes back exactly as originally recommended.**
The bottleneck was never the RM's judgment. It was how that judgment moved through
the organization.

By 2035, every bank will have access to similar AI. What compounds into real advantage
is an organization that recognizes, trusts, and routes to good judgment - consistently,
not just when a title says so.

## The Idea: Calibre

**Calibre** - how good someone's judgment turns out to be, tracked continuously over time.

Axis Calibre keeps every employee's decision track record live, and uses it - alongside
current capacity - to route each new case directly to whoever is best placed to decide
it, regardless of rank. A 3-year Officer with a strong track record can out-earn a
12-year veteran's discretion on a given case. Same titles. Same pay. The only thing
that changes is *who gets asked first.*

**AI recommends and routes cases. It never approves them.** Every decision is still made
by a person, and still stays inside Axis's existing RBI-compliant sanctioning framework -
governance and fairness are independently audited on top of it.

## How It Works

A closed feedback loop, not a one-time reassignment:

**Customer request → AI matches track record + capacity → best-suited person decides → outcome updates the record**

![How Axis Calibre Works](assets/visuals/how_it_works_loop.svg)

## Why It Matters - and Why It Compounds

| Stakeholder | What changes |
|---|---|
| **Customer** | Speed to Decision - how fast complex cases get resolved, end to end |
| **Axis** | Decision Quality Score - how accurate those decisions turn out to be, adjusted for difficulty |
| **Employee** | Rising Talent Rate - % of real discretionary authority held by employees who earned it early, not by tenure |
| **Organization** | Institutional Memory Reuse (called "Memory Reuse Rate" in the short deck) - % of new complex cases solved using a past decision's captured reasoning |

The combined effect we're aiming at: **top-talent retention**, because the organization
visibly recognizes and trusts judgment rather than waiting on it.

*(These are the proposed, expected outcomes we're designing toward at this stage - not
measured pilot results. See [`case_study/05_impact_and_metrics.md`](case_study/05_impact_and_metrics.md)
for how we'd validate them.)*

## Rollout Pathway

1. **Pilot** - SME lending, 2–3 regions, run in parallel ("dual-run") against the existing approval grid
2. **Expand** - extend to retail credit and risk officers; formalize governance based on pilot learnings
3. **Enterprise** - portable track records, organization-wide; illustratively extensible to Fraud, Corporate Banking, Treasury, Collections, and Customer Disputes

## Risks We've Named Up Front

- **Gaming the system** → difficulty-adjusted scoring, no case self-selection, mandatory case-mix diversity checks, and blind quarterly audits, so easy wins can't inflate a score
- **Regulatory fit** → the model operates entirely inside Axis's existing RBI-compliant sanctioning grid; nothing about the approval framework itself changes
- **Organizational resistance** → pay and titles are untouched; this changes *routing*, not compensation or hierarchy
- **A well-calibrated employee still causing a large loss** → hard, board-approved risk ceilings exist independently of track record; calibration only expands discretion within them
- **The system quietly drifting back to seniority-as-usual** → the Governance Committee runs a standing, published audit of whether Rising Talent Rate is genuinely shifting toward merit
